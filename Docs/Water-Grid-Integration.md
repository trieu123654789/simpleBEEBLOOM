## Implemented model

`Experiments/LetItBee-HoneyPilot/GAMA/LetItBeeWater.gaml` adds optional water actions to the existing model:

- `configure_water(water_json)` validates a complete domain before accepting it. Repeating the exact configuration does not reset water. A changed domain requires a new experiment.
- `set_water_running(running)` controls automatic simulation; default is stopped.
- `read_water` produces the last authoritative `water_state` snapshot.
- `advance_water(seconds)` advances an isolated diagnostic by up to ten simulated seconds. Not exposed as a SIMPLE client operation.

The model uses a regular Cartesian grid with inactive land cells. Storage is depth-averaged: each wet cell has a fixed volume = cell size squared times depth. Each internal face transfers salt conservatively with first-order upwind advection. Four-neighbour diffusion transfers equal and opposite salt mass. No row wrap, diagonal transport or transport through dry cells is allowed.

Discharge is prescribed in m3/s, not calculated from terrain or tides. Every cell's incoming and outgoing discharge must balance. Boundary faces use index -1 for the exterior reservoir and are allowed only on the rectangular domain perimeter. Their salt exchange is tracked explicitly. This is a salt-transport model with a prescribed flow field, NOT a momentum/free-surface hydrodynamic solver. It does not represent vertical stratification or a salt wedge.

An adaptive substep limits total outgoing transport to 45% of cell volume. Configurations requiring substeps below 0.001 seconds are rejected. Salt is not clipped to hide numerical errors. The diagnostic reports `mass_error_kg = current mass - initial mass - net boundary input`.

Scientific units: `cell_size_m`, `depth_m`, `discharge_m3_s`, `diffusivity_m2_s`, `salinity_g_l`. Numerically, 1 g/L = 1 kg/m3. Current Unity world-unit-to-metre scaling is an explicit assumption, not surveyed geography. New authoring controls use English names.

## Geometry survey and files

Unity menu: **Let It Bee > Water > Export Current Water Survey**.

The exporter reads enabled, explicitly marked ground colliders and the one active water renderer. It samples cell centres and four near-corners against the ground. All five must have known submerged ground before a cell is accepted. Missing bottom data is blocked, never assigned an invented depth. This conservative coarse mask can close narrow channels; it is not an exact shoreline reconstruction.

Current survey: 56 x 55 cells, 8 world units per cell, origin X/Z (-56,-64), 734 wet cells, 218 cells with unknown coverage/bottom, 405 mixed shoreline cells. Categories can overlap. It has six disconnected wet components; the largest has 626 cells. This requires review/refinement before Story use.

- `Water/gameplay-domain-survey.json`: actual sampled geometry, zero salinity and no prescribed flow. `review_required=true`.
- `Assets/Settings/LetItBee/Water/gameplay-domain-survey.json`: matching Unity TextAsset generated by the exporter.
- `Water/channel-demo.json`: synthetic straight channel for quick transport checks. Not the gameplay river.
- `Water/gameplay-flow-demo.json`: largest sampled wet component cropped to 47 x 53 cells. A 55-cell connected path carries prescribed flow from the lower-Z to upper-Z domain boundary. Inputs 0.2 m3/s, 15 g/L, D=0.2 m2/s are explicitly hypothetical. This demonstrates mapping and transport only; it does not infer the real river current. Other connected cells mix by diffusion.

The flow demo is a development fixture, not automatically regenerated when terrain changes. Export again and rebuild/review the flow fixture after edits. Never mistake a matching `domain_id` alone for proof that geometry did not change; the server compares the complete configuration.

## Viewing in GAMA

Open `GAMA/LetItBeeWaterReview.gaml`, run **WaterReview**. The default file is the synthetic channel. Enable **Run salt transport**, then Play. Cyan-to-pink is a concentration visualization, not the literal colour of salty water. White arrows indicate prescribed internal flow direction, not measured speed.

To inspect the actual surveyed river, choose `Water/gameplay-domain-survey.json` as **Water configuration JSON before starting a fresh experiment**. It will remain fresh/still by design. For the hypothetical river transport test, choose `Water/gameplay-flow-demo.json`. Check the configuration monitor says `accepted=true` before interpreting the display. An invalid file must not be mistaken for an empty river.

To mirror a running gameplay experiment instead, set **Live water checkpoint (optional, read only)** to the session's `Runtime/checkpoints/<session>.json.water.json`. This mode reads the authoritative salt field and cannot run its own simulation. Start/Play the viewer to refresh. The status explicitly reports LIVE or STALE based on the saved timestamp. A new domain requires restarting the viewer; a partial write retains the previous frame.

## Unity and SIMPLE

`BeeWaterGrid` is installed on `LetItBee Honey Pilot` in the gameplay scene with the survey TextAsset and both `Connect To Gama` and `Run Transport` OFF. **Let It Bee > Water > Select Water Diagnostics** selects/creates it without moving objects. It uses the existing serialized SIMPLE RPC loop and never registers another player. The modern SIMPLE envelope exposes water configuration/control/snapshots separately from the honey state. A missing/outdated water service reports a water status without erasing honey progress.

Select the component in Scene view to see the diagnostic grid with Gizmos enabled. During Play, accepted GAMA concentrations colour the cells; stale samples are gray. This is a Scene-view diagnostic, NOT a replacement material or an in-headset water effect. The production SIMPLE shader is untouched. Snapshot dimensions, origin, cell size, surface height and domain are checked before display.

Mapping: cell `(column,row)` centre = Unity `(originX+(column+.5)*size, surfaceY, originZ+(row+.5)*size)`. GAMA model coordinates use the same X/Z values as X/Y; only the diagnostic drawing flips vertical screen orientation.

Water retries preserve state. When configured through the gameplay model, a separate `<honey-checkpoint>.water.json` saves the environmental state on reads and approximately every simulated second while running. A new experiment restores only a matching, valid configuration/state; mismatch fails closed and does not overwrite the old file. Running resumes only when requested. Honey checkpoints remain independent. The standalone WaterReview demo has no persistence by default; its optional live-file mode is a read-only observer.

## Environment background and readable gameplay markers (2026-11-06)

HoneyPilotMap now reads `Water/gameplay-observation-map.json`. This is a **visual observation export**, separate from the conservative salt transport domain. The current export samples ground colliders against the one enabled horizontal water renderer every 2 Unity world units. Row strips preserve the sampled shoreline; unknown ground remains blank. It does not alter meshes, transforms, the transport grid, or saved gameplay parameters. The 2-unit sampling is an approximation, not the original mesh outline.

The export contains 54 nipa clusters, one coconut palm and two houses from the current scene. Nipa symbols use the root X,Z; the coconut and house symbols use visible renderer-bounds centers because imported pivots can be offset. House rectangles are axis-aligned renderer bounds, not exact building footprints. This is a top-down plan: altitude is not drawn, and seven closely spaced hive slots share one marker at their centroid with a stored/capacity label. Individual slot identities and snap positions remain unchanged. Flower markers are smaller; their coordinates are never spread apart artificially.

Salt colors apply only where a fine visual water strip maps to a valid wet transport cell. Blue-gray shoreline strips outside that domain have **no salt data**; they are not assumed fresh. Gray still means stale salt data. `Show environment background` and `Show salinity layer` allow isolating the layers. The original WaterReview diagnostic remains available.

To update the background after environment authoring, use **Let It Bee > Water > Export Observation Map Background** in Unity Edit Mode, then reinitialize the GAMA HoneyPilot viewer. The exporter does not save the scene. Its timestamp and whether the scene was unsaved are recorded and displayed, so an Editor export must not be mistaken for the installed APK layout. No new APK is required for these GAMA presentation changes; moving the actual environment still requires the normal build/deploy workflow.
